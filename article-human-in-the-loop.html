The Human Layer

Where the analyst still wins

By Pallav Pradhan · ~3 min read

There's a version of this story where machine learning arrives at credit and the analyst is finished. It's the wrong story, and I keep hearing it, so it's worth pushing back on: the reason I want to be the one standing between the model and the loan is not that I'm faster or that I don't trust the machine. It's that the exception is where all the value — and all the risk — actually lives.

A well-built model is brilliant at the middle ninety percent: the clean files, the obvious rejects, the routine yeses. It's genuinely bad at the ten percent that decide whether a portfolio is good or a disaster — the one that looks fine but the cash flow doesn't, the structure that's technically allowed but commercially reckless, the “everything checks out” that quietly checks out in a loop. That ten percent is where reading, not predicting, has to happen, and it's the part a model was not built to own.

“AI handles the volume. Judgement handles the exception. The exception is the job.”

The skill that doesn't get automating

The highest-value thing an underwriter can do is read the pattern the model half-sees, and the context it can't see at all — the market shift, the relationship, the reason a borrower structured the file the way they did, and whether that reason holds. A model will surface the signal; it takes a person to know whether it's a threat, an opportunity, or just noise, and to be accountable for that call when the money moves. That's not a job a black box can sign off on, and it shouldn't be. The model makes the reading faster and broader; the analyst decides what it means. And that decision is the one that actually makes a difference.

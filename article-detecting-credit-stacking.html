Detecting credit-stacking before it blows up

By Pallav Pradhan · ~4 min read

The fastest-growing liability in retail credit is the one a borrower never lists on the application. It isn't a single big debt — it's credit-stacking: five small BNPL instalments, three fintech micro-loans, a couple of instant-paylater balances, none of them individually scary, all of them quietly compounding into an obligation that no single line on the form reveals. On paper the file looks fine. In reality the borrower is already over-levered before your loan lands.

This is a data problem first and a judgement problem second, which is exactly why it's where a model earns its keep. The tell-tale signals are scattered and unglamorous: a spike in micro-lender enquiries in the bureau pull, repayment behaviour that's slipping across apps at the same time, utilisation ratios that don't match the income, and — the one I rely on most — transaction patterns that loop back on themselves. A model trained to read that combination can flag the stack early, while a human scanning the same file under deadline will usually only see the application the borrower chose to show.

“The risk isn't in any one small debt. It's in the sum of the small debts nobody told you about.”

What we do about the flag

Detection is only half the job. A useful system tells the underwriter what to look at and why it's worried — “elevated BNPL utilisation across three lenders, last 60 days” — rather than just a red dot. That's what makes it a tool instead of a black box: the analyst keeps the decision, and the model just makes the invisible obligation visible. That's the whole point, and it's the difference between catching the stack early and learning about it in the default reports a year later.

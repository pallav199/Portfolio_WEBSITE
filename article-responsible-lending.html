Responsible Lending

AI that lends safely, not just faster

By Pallav Pradhan · ~4 min read

The most common framing for AI in lending is a speed story: faster decisions, fewer queues, more volume through the funnel. That's real, and it matters. But it's the cheap argument, and the one that quietly leads banks to optimise for throughput at the expense of safety — which is precisely how you end up with a portfolio that grows fast and rots faster.

My rule is that speed and safety should never trade off, and that means treating three things as first-class properties of the model, not compliance checkboxes you add at the end: affordability — does the repayment actually fit the cash flow once the undisclosed obligations are in; explainability — can I tell the customer, and the regulator, why this decision in one or two sentences; and audit-readiness — can a year from now, a stranger reconstruct exactly what the model saw and what it decided?

“If you can't explain the decision in one sentence to the borrower, you don't have a lending model. You have a coin flip with extra steps.”

Why this is a model-design choice, not a legal one

A bank can absolutely build a model that clears every regulatory bar and still be the wrong tool — because a decision it can't explain, or a limit it sets a customer can't afford, is a liability with a polite label on it. The responsible version is one where the “reason code” is designed in, not scraped off later. That's a small difference in engineering and a huge difference in outcome: it's the difference between a bank that can defend every sanction in a regulator's eyes, and one that can defend the average.

For the customer, it's even simpler. A fast yes is a good thing; a fast yes that turns out to be unaffordable is a bad thing, and it costs more than the time you saved. That's the line I try to hold — the model should make the safe decision fast, not just make a decision fast.
